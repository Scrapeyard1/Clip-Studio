# Apologetics shorts: evidence, editing rules, and test plan
Research date: October 13, 2026
Prepared for Elijah Johnson Apologetics / Clip Studio

## Main finding

Build a system that finds and preserves a complete interesting exchange, then chooses the appropriate presentation. The four supplied videos demonstrate different visual approaches. Additional public examples show narrower topic packaging, different durations, and repurposing across long and short formats. They do not establish that any editing formula always wins.

This is a bounded research pass, not a full audiovisual or statistical study. Public video downloads and YouTube caption requests failed in this environment. YouTube storyboard images also could not be decoded. Consequently, external YouTube examples below support metadata and source-link observations, NOT claims about their exact cuts, zooms, music, retention, or sound effects. The supplied videos were visually sampled in the preceding review; their audio was not auditioned. One additional TikTok's native timestamped captions and cover were retrieved. Its moving video was unavailable. No exact source-to-short audiovisual alignment was completed for the external pairs.

The deliverable includes a usable Claude instruction set and an offline validation/timeline helper. It is not a fitted virality model, a finished renderer, or a claim of exhaustive niche research.

## Evidence categories

- V: sampled frames of user-supplied local video.
- T: timestamped native platform captions, with ASR limitations.
- M: native public title, duration, description, or other metadata.
- L: source relationship explicitly linked in a published description.
- C: candidate relationship supported by same channel/topic or a third-party link, but not aligned against footage.
- H: proposed implementation or editing hypothesis to test on Elijah's content.

## Creator practice and platform evidence

A [Christianity Today interview](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2025/08/cliffe-knechtle-apologetics-tiktok-campus-debates/) reports Stuart repurposing campus/TV material for TikTok, learning from uneven performance, and balancing broadly engaging issues with gospel-focused clips. This supports an iterative selection strategy, not an always-successful formula. It is a reported creator account, not a controlled experiment.

The ministry itself separates [full episodes](https://givemeananswer.org/watch-all-gmaa-videos/gmaa-tv-episodes/) and [social shorts](https://givemeananswer.org/watch-all-gmaa-videos/gmaa-youtube-shorts/). That is a useful product model: one source archive, several distinct outputs, with a path back to the fuller conversation.

[TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/creative-codes) recommends vertical production, space for interface controls, narrative structure, purposeful visual stimulation, and sound. Its evidence concerns advertising. Applying those principles to organic apologetics is a design hypothesis, not a proven niche-specific causal result. This kit uses restrained effects and prioritizes speech rather than copying an advertising CTA formula.

[YouTube's Shorts guide](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070?hl=en) distinguishes public starts/replays from engaged views. [Its content analytics definitions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en) distinguish staying to watch, average viewing duration, and average percentage viewed. Those measurements answer different questions. A high public view count alone cannot establish that viewers understood the argument or stayed for the payoff.

[YouTube's three-minute guide](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en) documents square/vertical Shorts up to three minutes under its eligibility conditions. The kit therefore permits up to 180 seconds by default; it does not require using all that time. Copyright guidance retrieved from this page and the [rights-holder page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13053317?hl=en) was inconsistent about automatic blocking of claimed Shorts over one minute. We do not encode that disputed behavior as a permanent rule. Use approved audio and check current upload results and platform policy.

## Selection and editing decisions for Clip Studio

1. Map full conversations first, then identify complete short-form arguments inside them. The current repository's single transcript-planning request needs a context-aware multi-pass workflow.
2. Produce separate lanes: quick reversal, substantive objection/response, Scripture explanation, and pastoral answer. Do not force one pacing preset on all four.
3. Require source evidence for editorial claims. A clip being exciting is not proof that a guest conceded.
4. Prefer chronological cuts. Reordering requires a deliberate review of causality and context.
5. Make speaker regions and evidence layouts explicit. Largest-face selection is insufficient.
6. Treat sound as its own track with approved assets and reversible cue placement. Transcript-only planning cannot locate soundboard effects reliably.
7. Keep source time and output time separate. Generate captions, effect positions, and exports from the same compiled timeline.
8. Return editable drafts automatically. Approval happens after the draft, with all decisions reversible.

These are proposed engineering/editorial rules (H), informed by the limited evidence above. Their numeric thresholds are not discovered universal laws.

## Measuring “winning” on Elijah's channel

Define success before selecting thresholds. Suggested goals: engaged viewing, shares/saves, subscribers or qualified movement to longform, and an argument represented accurately. Track correction rates and viewer confusion as costs. An angry comment count is not sufficient evidence of success.

Record each short's source, topic, preset, duration, opening, layout, effects, publish time, and version. At fixed ages (for example 24 hours and 7 days), compare with the channel's own recent clips in similar duration/topic cohorts. Keep YouTube and TikTok metrics separate. Record exactly which platform metric supplied each value; don't treat replay starts as unique viewers.

Inspect three questions: Did the opening earn attention? Did viewers reach the payoff? Did the clip create a useful next action? Use retention data where available, not just a single completion percentage. Separate failures of topic selection from failures of execution.

Test one variable at a time across suitable future posts: opening, duration, or presentation. Sequential posts are not randomized experiments; timing and distribution can confound results. Avoid reuploading near-identical clips repeatedly just to chase a score. Require a reasonable matched sample and an editor's review before making a tentative preference a default. All scoring weights in this kit begin as judgment-based priorities.

## Next evidence needed for a stronger model

A full paired audit needs playable original lives, corresponding finished shorts, and ideally the editor's project files. For each pair, align dialogue to the source, log actual removed ranges, speaker crops, caption treatment, source inserts, sound cues, and the eventual performance. Start with 10–20 matched pairs from Elijah's own channel, including mediocre results, so the model learns failures as well as selected favorites. This initial package does not claim that dataset already exists.
